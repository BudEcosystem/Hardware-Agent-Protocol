# Standing controller attribution

The large poll body is not an LTO-created duplication of the controller. Its failed-map section is **7462 bytes in both builds**. The retained pre-LTO fat-object and LTRANS listings contain the same 3580 instruction sequence after local-label renumbering. That comparison concerns these retained listings; the fit parent supplies exact source identity and map sizes, not a new object-code execution. Standing handle changes from 5816 to 5784 bytes, a 32-byte reduction. The finite pair contributes another 96-byte reduction; the four large bodies account for only 128 of the combined profile's 1172-byte flash reduction.

WPA records `loss_service`, `accept_observation` and `feedback` inlined into poll; `transition` into `evaluate`, then the sole `evaluate` caller into poll. It similarly inlines the sole `mutation` caller into handle, together with open, validation and response helpers. The compiler's IR size estimates are not AVR byte counts. A helper disappearing after single-caller inlining does not establish duplicate engines or promise savings from reversing that inlining.

The text-only parser reconciles each complete function's emitted instruction widths plus inline table data to its failed-map section. [attribution.json](attribution.json) preserves all static call sites and original assembly locations. These counts are static sites, not execution frequencies:

| Body | Instructions | Direct call sites | 64-bit arithmetic/comparison calls | Encoded 64-bit reads / writes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Standing poll | 3580 | 148 | 44 | 24 / 8 |
| Standing handle | 2733 | 124 | 3 | 8 / 17 |

The poll's 44 arithmetic sites are 27 `__cmpdi2`, five `__cmpdi2_s8`, six `__adddi3` and six `__subdi3`. Their call instructions occupy 176 bytes; operand preparation and spills occupy additional code that is not assigned wholesale to those helpers. The 24/8 serializer calls alone occupy 128 bytes, while their shared helper bodies are counted once in the image. Handle also contains two eight-entry switch tables, totaling 32 bytes, and two `jmp __tablejump2__` transfers; those jumps are not counted as calls.

Poll allocates 100 local bytes and saves 18 registers before its ordinary/loss branch. The complete body contains 448 `adiw/sbiw r28,<offset>-<base>` instructions around frame accesses above `Y+63`: **896 static instruction bytes**, about 12% of the section. This is measured address-adjustment code, not a claim that all 896 bytes can be removed. Many adjustments surround spilled eight-byte timestamps and widened bounds. Two `dispatch_report` sites each push 16 argument bytes; that helper then saves 18 registers and allocates 15 local bytes. Its three 64-bit timestamp parameters and operand movement are a concrete ABI cost to investigate. None of these observations is a combined stack bound.

The accepted-observation commit has one concrete duplicate expansion. [commit-blocks.json](commit-blocks.json) retains the demand-path instructions at raw lines 16428–16480 and feedback-path instructions at 16523–16575: an identical 48-instruction / 96-byte prefix copies the four observation fields and stores quality/continuity/pending markers. The paths then choose different readiness flags. Source lines 545–547 contain only one commit block; the compiler duplicated it after channel-specific validation. Outlining may share that prefix, but argument setup and the new call can offset savings; 96 bytes is duplicated emitted content, not a measured net saving.

The codec units also show bounded duplication: `read64` and `get64` are separate 48-byte bodies with identical instruction sequences modulo their local loop label; `read32` and `get32` likewise duplicate 34-byte bodies. `r64` aliases `read64`, `r32` aliases `read32`, and `w64` aliases `put64`. The shared 92-byte `put64` loops through `__lshrdi3`; each 64-bit reader loops through `__ashldi3`. These are real instructions, but runtime loop iteration counts must not be multiplied into flash bytes. The two duplicate read bodies account for 82 bytes before considering any replacement's linkage or calling costs.

The next candidates are bounded and require separate source review and measurement:

1. Preserve encoded copies as bytes: `w64(destination,r64(source))` is an exact eight-byte big-endian round trip. `memmove(destination,source,8)` preserves even overlapping snapshot semantics; a verified disjoint case may use `memcpy`. Fill constant `ABSENT` fields with eight `0xff` bytes. Keep every callback, validation, mutation and timestamp-publication boundary in its original order. Do not apply this rule to arithmetic or native `now_tick` serialization. It may introduce library code, so no net saving is assumed.
2. Reduce `dispatch_report`'s by-value timestamp arguments only after proving the replacement reads the same post-driver `endpoint->now_tick` with no intervening callback. Its current before/after/cutoff comparisons and strict cutoff must remain exact; encoded or pointer arguments need their own alias/lifetime proof and edge controls. Do not remove freshness checks or assume an earlier clock sample remains valid.
3. Consider a narrowly retained observation-commit helper for the proven 96-byte duplicate and a common private codec for the proven 82-byte duplicate bodies. Avoid a blanket no-inline change: large single-caller helpers alone are not duplication evidence.
4. Treat the independently observed retained finite frame on standing dispatch as a separate stack issue. A deliberate out-of-line `poll_finite` boundary could enforce the source's intended frame lifetime, but generated instructions and the complete graph must verify it; neither its stack nor flash effect has been measured here.

These findings do not identify a measured way to remove the remaining 9124 physical flash bytes. Target, protocol, 64-bit time domain, lifecycle/feedback/loss semantics, explicit roots and all reservations remain unchanged. No C or build-runner edit, compiler query, binary inspection tool, probe, target or emulator ran for this attribution.
