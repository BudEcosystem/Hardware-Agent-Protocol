# Serial framing and small-board evidence

The complete retained RFC 1662 text (1,440 LF lines, including appendices/references and ASCII diagrams), its current errata page (62 lines) and the UNO R3 manual (539 lines) were read. All four substantive board diagrams and relevant feature/pin tables were visually inspected on seven retained page renders. Exact hashes/ranges are in `research/constrained/reading-serial-full.json`. Linked papers, chip datasheets, electrical schematics and hardware security documentation were not thereby read.

[RFC 1662](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1662.txt) specifies PPP HDLC-like framing, not a hardware-operation protocol. Its octet-oriented framing separates boundary flags from escaped data, calculates FCS over decoded fields and discards invalid frames. Section 4.4 explains why relying on a prior closing flag after noise can lose the next frame; always opening frames simplifies recovery. Its ACCM defaults and negotiation are PPP-specific, and its bit-stuffed alternative is a different link mode. The proposed HMS candidate borrows the flag/escape/FCS mechanism while using a separate header and requiring a raw binary route; it does not claim PPP conformance.

FCS is detection of accidental corruption. The RFC's own security section warns that an unseen change of physical connection can create an insertion attack. A serial device path or checksum cannot authenticate equipment or prove current authority. Eventual session enrollment and physical command admission remain separate protocol obligations. The [current errata record](https://www.rfc-editor.org/errata/rfc1662) contains verified erratum 542, correcting the STD number for the RFC 1661 reference; it does not alter this framing arithmetic.

The [UNO R3 manual](https://docs.arduino.cc/resources/datasheets/A000066-datasheet.pdf) retrieved here is a fourteen-page document modified 03/11/2026, with revision history entry 20/10/2026. Search results referred to an older twenty-six-page copy. Source hashes and actual contents therefore matter more than a cached snippet. The processor's stated 2 kB SRAM, 32 kB flash and 1 kB EEPROM motivate a gateway/endpoint split and fixed memory budgets, not an automatic claim that a runtime fits.

The manual has limitations: page 5 leaves maximum power consumption as `xx`; page 2 says up to16 MHz while the generic processor paragraph on page 6 says up to20 MHz. Do not use those mixed scopes to invent an HMS timing qualification. The power-tree labels are not measured system consumption or validated actuator supply limits. The diagram and pin tables are sufficient orientation for research, not a commissioned wiring plan. Board electrical limits, oscillator/timer behavior, EEPROM endurance and reset sequencing still require the exact chip/board documentation and measurements.

The official R3/R4 comparison page was visible through the browser research tool but direct local acquisition returned403. The failed attempt remains in the manifest; no full local copy/read is claimed. No account was created or access restriction bypassed. The binding derives its small-board memory motivation from the successfully archived manual instead.

Original design deductions: cap decoded storage independently of untrusted length; recover from malformed/oversized frames at a fresh delimiter; give each feed at most one bounded event; require an external absolute receive deadline; keep partial receive state separate from motion state; and do not let low-priority serialization block the only stop mechanism. Separate framing, operation identity, authority and physical evidence even when all share one serial cable. The framing implementations are original code; no research repository was executed or incorporated as a runtime dependency.
