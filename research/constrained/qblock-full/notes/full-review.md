## HMS critique and proposed decisions

1. **Atomicity.** Complete transfer receipt and method response do not define durable HMS admission, an atomic multi-step physical transaction or rollback. Even if an adapter commits a resource only after complete assembly, the procedure's effects can remain non-atomic. Stage inert bytes; validate digest/schema/budgets and exact bindings; admit a separate immutable operation. A final block or Continue cannot start an instruction prefix.
2. **Loss and replay.** The protocol recovers blocks and can continue despite lost feedback. Missing acknowledgment never proves that an action did not occur. A new CoAP token or Request-Tag is not a new HMS intent. Retain durable operation identity and original timing across fallback, reupload, reconnect and response recovery. If earlier effect is possible, reconcile rather than dispatch a fresh action.
3. **Assembly.** Exact sizes improve completeness detection but are attacker-controlled until validated. Set byte/offset/block limits before allocation, reject inconsistent size/format/tag, do not overwrite duplicates, and require complete byte coverage. Bind staged bytes to principal, context, method/resource and generation. An ETag is representation identity, not a cryptographic content digest or authorization.
4. **Streaming.** Recovery of some missing notification blocks does not retain every sample. Resource replacement can make an old body irrecoverable. Preserve a gap or supersession record and acquisition age, not only receipt age. For complete scientific records, use immutable data references plus a retained application index/cursor; page an unbounded stream into bounded objects. A digest validates bytes, not truth of physical provenance or freshness.
5. **Cancellation.** Forgetting tokens and eventual RST-based deletion is transfer cleanup with no bounded physical-stop claim. A canceled transfer may already have completed at the peer. Mark its generation closed, reject late data into a successor, and preserve unresolved operation claims. Cancelling future procedure dispatch and stopping already active hardware require their separate authorized paths and evidence.
6. **DoS and timing.** Default backoff/retention can exceed useful decision or latest-start windows. Bound total bodies, tags, retained tokens, missing-list parser work, restart/retry budgets and absolute duration, with aggregate per-peer/global quotas. Enforce protocol congestion pacing while scheduling control/watchdog work independently. Do not treat trusted OSCORE content as unlimited allocation permission. Deadline expiry ends eligibility, not network congestion controls.
7. **OSCORE and gateways.** Inner integrity and Outer fragmentation are independent. A manipulated Outer M bit can amplify retransmission without forging inner payload. Select hop protection and quotas, preserve protected Request-Tag/body-integrity rules, and avoid conflating security-context replay state with durable operation deduplication. New proxy tags cannot reorder or authorize procedures. Distinct staged object identities avoid mutable-resource upload replacement as accidental control policy.
8. **Qualification gap.** The review supplies a binding proposal and acceptance cases, not a Q-Block parser/server, operating system integration, secure deployment, MCU fit, physical execution, recovery proof or real-time guarantee. Referenced RFCs/DOTS work not explicitly read in this lane remain outside its new coverage. The wider literature task remains open.
